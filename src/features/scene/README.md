# Scene

Optional local blocking tool at `/[locale]/scene`, reached from Your cast. It does not change actor data or download/account rules. Generic stand-ins are explicitly not delivered actor models. No generation, uploads, cloud saves or animation player. Five optional handles adjust a selected stand-in by hand.

- `index.ts` is the server page entry; `SceneEditor` owns plain versioned draft/history and UIKit controls. Only its `dynamic(..., {ssr:false})` canvas imports Three/Fiber/Drei. Cast links disable prefetch. Do not export the canvas through a shared site/client barrel.
- `model.ts` owns pose/build IDs, ready layouts, framing requests and local draft validation. Lazy `framing.ts` measures the loaded, scaled and turned posed meshes, then uses CameraControls' distance solver on camera-oriented bounds (its `fitToBox` snaps to cardinal axes in 3.1.2). Full includes the raised hands and number; Medium/Close-up crop the actual posed upper body. Heights are centimetres; ground x/z are metres, Y is elevation, +Z is forward. Missing actor height starts at an explicitly approximate 175 cm. Body width changes use distinct prepared assets, not a whole-body horizontal stretch.
- The WebGL surface has a fixed neutral export palette, independent of the UI theme. Preview and PNG share the CameraControls camera, aspect, attached number markers and coloured floor bands. Number badges remain readable at phone preview sizes. `figure-labels.ts` reprojects their original anchors, moves 20 px boxes clear of other labels and projected floor rings, up or sideways in 24 px steps and draws a 1.5 CSS px leader in that person's colour, with a 4 px dot at the head; preview and PNG each resolve at their actual output size. Shot (Full/Medium/Close-up), angle (Eye/Low/High/1→2 shoulder), 24/50/85 mm lens and subject (selected figure/everyone) are independent controls. The shoulder view uses figure 1 as foreground and frames figure 2; choosing everyone or another subject returns to Eye. The fixed horizontal sensor gate is 36 mm. A readable name/colour key sits below the preview; PNG appends a fixed-size, wrapping numbered actor-name key below the camera image so names cannot occlude figures or shrink with cast size; a caption records lens, shot, angle and subject. FRAMES dimensions describe the camera image; the key adds 64 px per row plus a 48 px caption below it. Save scene downloads the same v1 JSON as the local draft, including camera position/target/lens/shot/sensor/subject; this is not yet the shared MediaFactory rig/pose interchange. Colours identify slots in this draft, not actor brands. Copy prompt maps those same labels/colours to each actor's reference-sheet page; unassigned figures are explicitly marked. Demand rendering, DPR capped at 1.5; no post-processing or remote lighting. Export renders at the requested dimensions, omits grid/selection and restores preview sizing in `finally`.
- The first fold keeps four blocking presets (two talking, over shoulder, five and eight), PNG/JSON exports, person/pose/body and the helper text visible at 1440×900 and 390×844. Camera pills, precise placement and actor-reference prompt use native disclosure controls; the three-person layout remains in Fine-tune. The desktop inspector takes a side column only while open; otherwise it sits below the workspace. Save actions and history/reset actions are two groups, so phone reset never ends up alone. The preview is the exact selected aspect ratio, including phone 16:9. Compact controls sit above the desktop stage. On desktop the stand-in note shares the Pose by hand line; its activation hint stays available to screen readers and remains visible on phones. Saved-draft status uses a screen-reader output, and PNG success uses a UIKit toast inside the stage, centred 12 px above its bottom edge for 2 seconds (failures stay visible). Desktop selectors are 36 px high, and the compact Camera/Pose band leaves a 12–16 px gap above the stage. The compact band reserves 272 px of viewport height, keeping true 16:9 at full content width in the 1440×900 first view; shorter windows still constrain width. Layout buttons size to their text without wrapping. Phone and portrait/square height budgets constrain width instead of squashing the frame. Two-person presets set pose, spacing, shot, lens and subject together. On phones below 640 px, the eight-person preset stays in one row, ordered 1–8, with Medium at eye level and number anchors about 12% below the stage top. Mean centre spacing is 12.2% closer than desktop (about 15%, with extra elbow clearance at three gaps); the same ≤15% projected-overlap check covers this phone row. The camera moves down without changing the fitted row width, eliminating the former empty upper half. Legs now remain visible: the unchanged row width and 16:9 gate limit body-height fill to about 66%, not the requested 85%; retaining both that fill and a mid-thigh crop would require a different row/gate or distorted proportions. Measured shoulder widths are about 1.18× desktop, not the former two-row 1.5×. Full remains available; desktop keeps its original eight-person arc and Full camera. These are preset edits saved in the ordinary draft, not a different export-only composition. Close-up targets the upper chest with heads in the upper half, rather than fitting a wide gap between people. Clay, warm-neutral bevelled seats, soft foot/seat contact patches and a fading grid use a neutral studio palette. A screen-space studio sweep is about 9% darker at the top, blending to the existing floor colour by the horizon; no post-processing pass is added.
- `sp-scene-v1` saves one local draft (old camera fields receive defaults); Undo/Redo is session-only (30 edits). Automatic fit resolution replaces the pending camera within the same history entry; orbit/pan/zoom gestures are saved as edits. All requested actors enter directly without mutating the saved cast. There is no people cap; numbered identities remain unique when the eight band colours repeat. Ready layouts place 2/3/5/8 grounded figures; layouts reuse the first assigned actors and undo restores the previous group. Storage/WebGL failure leaves ordinary actor references and the Guide available.
- Setting references reuse `features/settings`, also used in Guide. Selected setting/sentence edits share this draft/history; old drafts receive defaults. References are not 3D backgrounds and never change the clean PNG. Delivered MediaFactory references support real PNG downloads; any future undelivered slot stays honestly empty. Ownership, grouped browsing and bounded integration checks are in `../settings/README.md`.

## Figure asset handoff

`loadFigure(build) → GLB URL` and `loadPose(pose) → JSON URL` in `figure-assets.ts` are the runtime seam. Only the lazy canvas requests the current bodies and poses; identical URLs share the loader cache, and changing a pose reuses the body GLB. `figures.json` records the six runtime standing skinned heights, measured after head refinement with the delivered stand pose. Actor gender selects the matching average body; an explicit known build can refine it, and missing information uses average. The website actor contract currently supplies gender and stature, not a build classification; no build is inferred from appearance.

MediaFactory commit `e7b91cd1b1aca3c4647ed968eceae6221b72ad04`, `handoff-rigged/`, supplies slim/average/broad and their `-f` variants plus twelve shared poses. Each body has one skinned mesh, the same 53 named bones, no textures or animation clips, and 7,478 male / 8,662 female triangles. Metres, +Y up, +Z forward. `rigged-figure.ts` uses SkeletonUtils.clone for independent skeletons, applies every local quaternion by bone name, updates skin bounds, and grounds the actual deformed vertices at y=0 after stature scaling. The loader source remains untouched. Pose changes and body swaps preserve the saved pose; old v1 draft IDs still migrate. Sitting adds one warm-neutral (#BDB6AC), softly bevelled stool under the posed pelvis, moving/scaling/turning with that figure and included in bounds and PNGs; no table or other prop is added. Preview culling and framing use refreshed deformed bounds. Static posed vertices are baked once into a CPU-only picking mesh, so pointer moves use accurate triangles without repeating bone transforms per triangle; hits retain the original skinned mesh identity, and the cache follows placement/turning and is disposed with that pose.

`figure-ik.ts` wraps Three.js `CCDIKSolver` with a private bone-array view, leaving the delivered 53-bone skeleton/bind data unchanged. Wrists and ankles use two-link chains with a positive elbow/knee hinge and the current hinge plane; head uses a private tip and limited head/neck chain. `LimbHandles` portals UIKit handles directly onto those bones so demand-rendered HTML projections cannot pile up at the floor origin. Dragging uses a camera-facing plane. Wrists add 20% gesture-relative clavicle follow, then orient the hand along the reach. Within 20 CSS px of another shoulder, a posed-skin raycast supplies its surface and outward normal; the wrist sits 25 mm outside it, fingers lie tangent and the palm faces the surface. Each completed/interrupted gesture stores named local quaternions in optional `figures[].joints` (old drafts default to `{}`), as one undo entry. Body/height swaps preserve these rotations, changing a shelf pose/preset clears them. On entry to limb mode (and active-person changes), desktop and phone both use Medium at eye level. For two people, fit their combined bounds at a 56% width gate with roughly 10% crown headroom, rather than the selected person alone. A soft elbow bend keeps low resting wrists reachable in that crop; both free wrists move 15 mm outward with fingers down and palms inward/slightly forward, clear of the thighs rather than spread across them. The hand-on-hip preset's left hand is also released in drag mode; otherwise its flat contact fingers still form an open C. Free finger/thumb rotations match the lineup's shared closed half-curl, with fingers together and each thumb alongside the index. Snapping a wrist onto a body restores build 10's delivered-plus-additive contact curl; leaving contact restores the free half-curl. Manual shoulder contact still takes precedence. Saved joint edits still take precedence. Put number badges beside the heads and the head handle on the forehead: CSS-pixel hit targets need more room on phones than on desktop. Larger groups still frame the selected person. Camera transforms stay unchanged during the gesture. All visible 14 px markers are hollow rings inside 32 px UIKit hit areas, so skin contact stays visible. Grounding remains that of the shelf pose so lifting a foot does not move the torso. Picking is re-baked when the edited pose is committed; handles never appear in the exported canvas.

`head-geometry.ts` keeps only one curved split on rigid-head edges longer than 25 mm, cached per source geometry. Build 14 uses MediaFactory's independently accepted closed-mouth heads, closed eyelids and tapered bob/bun rim. The former SP lip/cheek/nose deformation, tangent-normal override and female hair-cap inset are removed; every delivered vertex is retained unchanged. Runtime head subdivision and normal welding remain, with the same 10,000-triangle ceiling. `figures.json` is calibrated from each new runtime standing height; producer geometry is never edited. `figure-hands.ts` sets one shared closed half-curl for free hands in both lineup and drag mode (rather than adding more bend to the delivered curl), retaining the original build-10 curl separately for body contact, pins seated wrists on the actual upper thighs and tucks crossed fingers under the other biceps (palm up, fingers pointing back), using the existing local IK. Lean-in adds a clearer torso incline and opens both shoulders 10° away from the ribcage. Only #5 of an eight-person lineup with the lean-in pose lowers both clavicles 5°, closes both upper arms 6° lifts the chin 8°, extends the upper spine 5° and retracts the neck 5°; other lean-in uses stay unchanged. These shelf corrections run before persisted manual edits. The hand-on-hip shelf pose uses local IK to pin the left wrist 18 mm outside the skin at the hip crest, palm inward and fingers forward/down; persisted manual edits still take precedence. Five-person blocking puts the sitter 0.6 m front-left of the front standing row. #2 stands 0.3 m behind him, within arm's reach, and leans with her right palm on his visible left shoulder; #4 remains in a rear gap and uses the delivered stand shelf's contrapposto weight shift and soft knees, with both hands free. #2–4 turn 25–30° inward; #5 turns a further 30° (−60° total) toward the sitter, so he no longer reads as front-facing; both shoulders open 11° away from his back only in this seated five-person group (4° more than build 11 to clear the armpit), using the lean-in clearance method without changing other standing figures. The lazy canvas samples the sitter's actual shoulder skin, solves the neighbour's wrist 25 mm outside it before manual overrides, then bakes picking from that pose. Its acceptance bounds still conservatively limit projected horizontal body-box overlap to 15%.

The delivered [LICENSE.md](../../../public/scene/rigged/LICENSE.md), [producer manifest](../../../public/scene/rigged/manifest.json) and [apply-pose README](../../../public/scene/rigged/README.md) are copied unchanged beside the six GLBs and twelve pose JSONs. Every delivered body/pose SHA-256 and byte count is checked against that manifest, with metadata pinned in the acceptance test. The licence records MPFB 2.0.18, donor commit/package hash and CC0 asset-data provenance, excluding GPL add-on code; this README adds no broader licence claim. No actor likeness/model, Mannequin.js or GPL application code is shipped. The previous static delivery remains archived at `public/scene/figures/` for provenance and is never requested by the stage. Keep the **10,000-triangle** ceiling; the renderer licence does not clear a model.
